History of Biorhythm Theory

The biorhythm theory originated in the late 19th century, proposing that human life is governed by innate biological cycles that begin at birth. Although later considered pseudoscience, it remains a popular tool for personal reflection.

  • 1897: Austrian physician Wilhelm Fliess proposes the 23‑day, 28‑day and 33‑day cycles.
  • Early 1900s: The idea spreads through magazines, novels and public fascination.
  • 1970‑1990s: Peaks in self‑help and sports performance, later debunked by scientific studies.
  • Today: Used mainly for introspection, curiosity and occasional novelty.
